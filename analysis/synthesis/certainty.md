## Draft sentences for the Results (SWiM item 6; edit before use)

- **G1a.** The certainty of evidence that a single acuity-survey item identifies acuity-defined low vision or legal blindness with the sensitivity and specificity shown in Table [accuracy] was **low**: downgraded once for risk of bias (the items and their cut-offs were selected by AUC ranking among 100 items in the same 385 participants, QUADAS-2 index-test domain high) and once for indirectness (a research cohort recruited to span the acuity range, items answered inside the full survey, and an acuity-only reference standard that cannot register field loss). Inconsistency could not be assessed with one dataset; imprecision was not a concern (264 and 73 reference-positive participants); publication bias could not be assessed.
- **G1b.** For the two Statistics Canada HALS questions against presenting acuity, certainty was **very low**: one community study whose verification depended on the screening answers (partial verification, 45% participation), an older-adult population, and 12 or fewer reference-positive participants, so the confidence intervals span most of the sensitivity range (0.43-0.95; 0.40-1.00).
- **G1c and G2.** For the seven falls-risk questions of Labreche 2018, certainty was **very low** against the composite reference and against a peripheral field defect: a retrospective chart reference with contradictory threshold wording, an enriched over-65 clinic sample, and 31 (composite) or 10 (field) reference-positive participants.
- **G3a.** For the Spanish LVQOL cut-off, certainty was **very low**: a two-gate design contrasting impaired patients with younger healthy volunteers, a cut-off chosen by ROC in the same sample, and a reference that is group membership rather than rehabilitation need.
- **G3c.** For the correlations between full-length questionnaires and measured vision, certainty was **low** for studies of 100 or more participants and **very low** for smaller ones: no study pre-specified hypotheses, every COSMIN risk-of-bias rating was doubtful, and no instrument was tested against the same comparator in more than one independent sample.
- **G4.** No included study evaluated any questionnaire or item against clinician-judged need for, or referral to, low vision rehabilitation with a two-by-two table; this is the central gap in the evidence map.
- **G6.** For the Cerebral Vision Screening Questionnaire, certainty was **very low** and the evidence is indirect (stroke survivors, a guideline's re-tabulation of one primary study).
