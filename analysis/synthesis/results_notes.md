## G1a. Single acuity-survey item vs measured visual acuity (Wu 2026, ID 92; Wu 2024, ID 128)

1. **Evidence base.** One independent dataset in two reports (385 adults, four US sites, June 2019 to February 2022, mean age 55.7 years, 57.4% female, glaucoma 65 = 16.9%, macular degeneration 19.2%; ID 92 p2-3) evaluated single yes/no items of the 100-item acuity survey against binocular habitual-correction VA at two thresholds (Table accuracy, Figure forest, Figure ROC). Wu 2026 (ID 92) carries the accuracy results; Wu 2024 (ID 128) is the same cohort's item-response-theory development paper and contributes only the correlation of estimated with measured VA (r = -0.73; ID 128, Results section "Estimating VA From Estimated Vision Abilities"); the two are counted once.
2. **Finding, led by the only study.** In the one study (ID 92, n = 385, prevalence of VA 0.3 logMAR or worse 0.686 = 264/385, derived from Table 1 p3), "No" to Q97 "In a fast-food restaurant, can you usually read the posted menus?" had sensitivity 0.89 (95% Wilson CI 0.85-0.92) and specificity 0.81 (0.73-0.87) for low vision at 0.3 logMAR (point estimates ID 92 p4; CIs computed from the reconstructed 2x2, TP 235, FP 23, FN 29, TN 98). "No" to Q30 "When seated in front of a computer, are you able to read the letters on the keyboard keys?" had sensitivity 0.89 (0.80-0.94) and specificity 0.71 (0.66-0.76) for acuity-defined legal blindness at 1.0 logMAR (prevalence 0.190 = 73/385; ID 92 p4; 2x2 TP 65, FP 90, FN 8, TN 222). The paper's "AUC" values (0.85 and 0.80; ID 92 p4) are (sensitivity + specificity)/2 at a single cut point, by the authors' own statement (ID 92 p3), and are not ROC areas (§ in the table). Across the ten tabulated items per threshold, sensitivity ranged from 0.71 to 0.91 and specificity from 0.71 to 0.91 at 0.3 logMAR (Q15 newspaper 0.91 / 0.74; Q46 licence plate 0.79 / 0.91; ID 92 p4) and the top legal-blindness items ranged 0.71 to 0.89 sensitivity and 0.71 to 0.82 specificity (Q62 light switch 0.71 / 0.82; ID 92 p4). Adding a second item changed the top-10 mean AUC by 0.04 at each threshold (0.81 to 0.85; 0.77 to 0.81; ID 92 p4-5), the abstract's bound being "<= 0.05" (ID 92 p1); the best pair for low vision was Q97 + Q15 (sensitivity 0.86, specificity 0.88; ID 92 p5), for legal blindness Q30 + Q12 (0.88 / 0.77; ID 92 p5); a third item added nothing (ID 92 p5-6). **The two named items (Q97 and Q30) were never evaluated together as a pair** (ID 92 Table 5 p6; `paper/two_item_accuracy.md` section 2). PPV and NPV are given only as means over the top-10 sets: low vision PPV 0.93, NPV 0.58; legal blindness PPV 0.40, NPV about 0.95 (ID 92 p7), at this sample's prevalences, so they do not transfer to a clinic.
3. **Heterogeneity (within the one dataset).** The threshold explains the spread: at 1.0 logMAR the item is as sensitive (0.89) but less specific (0.71 vs 0.81), because the task (reading keyboard letters) sits near the 20/200 difficulty and many people with moderate low vision also answer "No" (FP 90 of 312 non-blind). Reference standard: binocular habitual-correction VA, better eye if only monocular was available, Bailey-Lovie, ETDRS or Snellen charts mixed (ID 92 p2). Setting and spectrum: a research cohort recruited to span the acuity range, with 68.6% at or worse than 0.3 logMAR (ID 92 p3) and 14% at a measurement ceiling (ID 92 p4). Administration: inside the 100-item survey, mode NR (ID 92 p2-3). No glaucoma subgroup result.
4. **Risk of bias and what it would do.** QUADAS-2 overall High (`quadas2_table.md`): the items, pairs and triplets were selected by AUC ranking over 100 items in the same 385 people with no validation sample (D2 High), which inflates both sensitivity and specificity by an unknown amount; patient selection, reference-standard blinding and the interval are Unclear (D1, D3, D4); applicability concerns High for patients (enriched spectrum) and for the index test (answered inside the full survey). The whole-survey predicted-VA classifier was less accurate than its best item at 0.3 logMAR (sensitivity 0.94, specificity 0.66; ID 92 p6) and failed at 1.0 logMAR (sensitivity 0.06, specificity 1.00; ID 92 p6; both extraction passes agree, and the 2x2 reconstruction flags the row as inconsistent, so the reviewer should check the page).
5. **Certainty.** Low for the sensitivity and specificity of each named item (downgraded for risk of bias and indirectness; inconsistency and publication bias could not be assessed; `certainty.md` G1a).
6. **Bottom line.** A single question about reading a posted fast-food menu identified acuity-defined low vision with sensitivity 0.89 and specificity 0.81, and a question about reading keyboard letters identified acuity-defined legal blindness with sensitivity 0.89 and specificity 0.71, in one enriched research cohort in which the items were both chosen and tested; neither item has been tested as a stand-alone screen, as a pair with the other, in a glaucoma clinic, or against need for rehabilitation.

## G1b. Two population-survey vision questions vs presenting acuity (Gresset 2002, ID 79)

1. **Evidence base.** One independent dataset: the Quebec community sample of the Canadian Study of Health and Aging (1,777 people aged 65 and over answered the two Statistics Canada HALS questions; 144 eligible for examination, 65 examined = 45.1% participation; ID 79 p3). Reference: presenting near or distance VA with usual correction, best eye, at 6/12 and at 6/21 (ID 79 p2-3).
2. **Finding.** In the 65 examined (prevalence 0.18 at 6/12, 12 positive; 0.06 at 6/21, 4 positive), a positive answer to either question ("trouble reading ordinary newsprint with glasses" or "trouble seeing clearly the face of someone 12 feet away") had sensitivity 0.83 (0.52-0.98) and specificity 0.83 (0.71-0.92) at 6/12, and sensitivity 1.00 (0.40-1.00) and specificity 0.75 (0.62-0.85) at 6/21 (ID 79 Table 4 p5; CIs from the 2x2). The reading question alone: 0.75 (0.43-0.95) / 0.81 (0.69-0.91) at 6/12; the distance question alone: 0.50 (0.21-0.79) / 0.98 (0.90-1.00). Both questions positive: 0.50 / 0.98 at 6/12 and 1.00 / 0.95 at 6/21 (ID 79 Table 4 p5).
3. **Heterogeneity.** Threshold: the stricter 6/21 criterion raised sensitivity to 1.00 on 4 positives and lowered specificity. Reference construct: presenting (not best-corrected) VA, near or distance, so uncorrected refractive error counts as impairment. Setting: population survey, prevalence 0.06-0.18, interviewer-administered.
4. **Risk of bias.** QUADAS-2 overall High: D1 and D4 High (verification depended on the screening answers; 45.1% participated), D2 and D3 Low (ETDRS chart, examiner blind to the answers; `summary.md`). Partial verification would inflate sensitivity; the authors say the estimates cannot be applied to an unselected population (ID 79 p5-6).
5. **Certainty.** Very low (risk of bias, indirectness, very serious imprecision; `certainty.md` G1b).
6. **Bottom line.** Two interviewer-asked survey questions flagged presenting acuity of 6/12 or worse in older community residents with sensitivity around 0.8 and specificity around 0.8, on 12 reference-positive people; the only brief self-report items in the review with a blinded, chart-based reference, but at very low certainty.

## G1c. Seven falls-risk vision questions vs composite impairment (Labreche 2018, ID 27)

1. **Evidence base.** One dataset: 89 adults over 65 recruited from three university optometry clinics, deliberately enriched (ID 27 p2, p4); reference: retrospective chart composite of VA, contrast sensitivity and field (Group 1), asymmetric acuity or ocular deviation (Group 2), peripheral field defect (Group 3) (ID 27 p2).
2. **Finding.** "Yes" to any of the seven questions (Q5 reversed) had sensitivity 0.88 (0.77-0.95) and specificity 0.48 (0.30-0.67) for any impairment (prevalence 0.67, 60/89; ID 27 Table 1 p3). Single items against the Group 1 composite (31 positive): Q2 reading with glasses 0.74 (0.55-0.86) / 0.86 (0.74-0.93); Q5 distinguishing facial features 0.52 (0.33-0.69) / 0.98 (0.90-1.00); Q6 seeing the television 0.48 (0.31-0.67) / 0.97 (0.88-0.99); Q4 going down steps 0.46 (0.25-0.61) / 0.90 (0.78-0.96) (ID 27 Table 1 p3; CIs partly from the 2x2, † where counts were back-calculated).
3. **Heterogeneity.** Reference construct: a chart-review composite whose VA threshold is printed two ways (Methods "VA < 0.1 logMAR", Table 1 footnote "VA > 0.1 logMAR"; ID 27 p2-3). Spectrum: enriched, two-thirds impaired. Administration: telephone up to 4 months after the examination (ID 27 p2). The TICS-M > 21 cognitive subgroup (n = 52) gave higher item sensitivities (0.57-0.87; ID 27 Table 1 p3) and is kept in the supplement.
4. **Risk of bias.** QUADAS-2 overall High: D3 High (retrospective composite with contradictory wording), D1, D2, D4 Unclear; applicability High for patients (over-65 falls-risk target). Abstract n (86) disagrees with Results (89).
5. **Certainty.** Very low.
6. **Bottom line.** A seven-question falls-risk screen was sensitive (0.88) but unspecific (0.48) for any charted impairment in enriched clinic patients; its single items are specific but miss half or more of impaired patients.

## G2. Single items vs visual field (Labreche 2018, ID 27)

1. **Evidence base.** One dataset (the same 89 patients), three items aimed at field loss, reference "peripheral field defect" by confrontation or any perimetric defect (10 positive, prevalence 0.11; ID 27 p2, Table 1 p3). **No other included study tested any single item against a visual field.**
2. **Finding.** Q3 "difficulty noticing objects off to the side" sensitivity 0.50 (0.20-0.80), specificity 0.86 (0.77-0.92); Q4 steps or curbs 0.40 (0.14-0.73) / 0.81 (0.70-0.89); Q7 tripping over unseen objects 0.20 (0.04-0.56) / 0.86 (0.76-0.93) (ID 27 Table 1 p3).
3. **Heterogeneity.** Not assessable within one dataset; the reference ("constriction" on confrontation or any perimetric defect) is loose.
4. **Risk of bias.** As G1c; with 10 field-positive participants every CI spans more than half the range.
5. **Certainty.** Very low.
6. **Bottom line.** The only direct test of self-report items against field loss found that half or more of patients with a field defect answered "No" to the field-oriented questions; self-report of peripheral loss is insensitive in this sample, which is the central caution for a glaucoma clinic.

## G3a. Full questionnaire cut-off vs acuity-defined group (SLVQOL, Pérez-Mañá 2022, ID 119)

1. **Evidence base.** One dataset: 170 visually impaired patients (best-eye BCVA 0.30 logMAR or worse at the public hospital, 0.52 or worse at the private clinic) and 195 healthy subjects (BCVA 0.10 or better in the worse eye) (ID 119 p2, p4).
2. **Finding.** A Spanish LVQOL total score of 105 or 106 (the paper gives both) separated the groups with sensitivity 0.954 and specificity 0.918 (ID 119 p1, p4, p9; CIs not reported; approximate 0.91-0.98 and 0.87-0.95 from back-calculated counts, †). Score correlated with best-eye BCVA at r = -0.404 (n = 170, ID 119 p4).
3. **Heterogeneity.** Not assessable; note the mode effect: 393.5 s by telephone vs 290.0 s face-to-face in impaired patients (ID 119 p5-6).
4. **Risk of bias.** QUADAS-2 overall High: D1 High (two-gate, controls younger), D2 High (cut-off chosen by ROC in the same sample; mode differed by site); the index test is a 25-item PROM, not a screen, and the reference is group membership, not LVR need.
5. **Certainty.** Very low.
6. **Bottom line.** A full LVQOL questionnaire can tell impaired patients from healthy volunteers almost perfectly, which is a different and easier task than finding, among eye-clinic patients, those who need rehabilitation.

## Why the two questions work and what they miss

This section joins `paper/two_item_accuracy.md`, `paper/candidate_items.md`, the feasibility table's field/contrast column and the QUADAS-2 D2 judgement.

**Why they work.** (a) Each item was keyed by item-response theory to a specific acuity threshold: the 100-item survey's item difficulties in logits map linearly onto logMAR (ID 92 p3), and the IRT ability estimated from all 100 items correlated r = -0.73 with measured VA (ID 128, Results), so an item whose difficulty sits at 0.3 or 1.0 logMAR is, by construction, a one-question acuity test. (b) The tasks are familiar, high-contrast, fixed-distance recognition tasks: a posted menu at a counter and keyboard letters at arm's length, which is why the acuity-matched items were the most accurate of the 100 (Q97 AUC 0.85 highest at 0.3 logMAR; Q30 0.80 highest at 1.0 logMAR; ID 92 p4). (c) The "No = positive" rule and the AND rule for pairs make the pair trade sensitivity for specificity in a predictable direction (pair specificity 0.88-0.94 vs single 0.81; ID 92 p5). (d) The practical gain over the whole survey is real: the 100-item classifier was less accurate (AUC 0.80 vs 0.85 at 0.3 logMAR; ID 92 p6-7) and takes far longer.

**What they miss, and how much to discount the numbers.**
1. *Derivation-sample optimism (QUADAS-2 D2 High).* The 20 best single items, 1,980 pairs and 1,960 triplets were ranked by AUC in the same 385 people that supply the reported accuracy, with no hold-out, split or external sample (ID 92 p3, p5; `two_item_accuracy.md` section 4). Selecting the maximum of 100 noisy estimates inflates the winner; the size of the inflation is unknown. The t-tests comparing top-10 lists are not valid inference for the same reason (`two_item_accuracy.md` section 7, item 9). The writer should call every figure "derivation-sample" and expect lower values on replication.
2. *Acuity only.* The reference standard is VA alone; the authors state that the items "do not detect legal blindness due to visual-field loss alone" and "may misclassify individuals with preserved acuity but severely constricted visual fields" (ID 92 p8). The feasibility table scores the two-item screen "none" for field/contrast coverage. The only direct test of self-report items against a field defect found sensitivities of 0.20-0.50 (ID 27 Table 1 p3), and the Activity Inventory mobility measure tracked the outer 10-30 degree field (rho 0.61) better than acuity (-0.27) (ID 46 p5), so field loss is both common in the target clinic and poorly self-reported.
3. *Context and mode.* The items were answered inside the 100-item survey, by a mode the paper does not state (ID 92 p2-3, p8); the authors warn performance "might differ if administered independently" (ID 92 p8). The named items depend on familiarity with fast-food menus and keyboards (ID 92 p8).
4. *Spectrum and prevalence.* 68.6% of the cohort had VA of 0.3 logMAR or worse (ID 92 p3); PPV 0.93 and NPV 0.58 for low vision (ID 92 p7) are properties of that prevalence. In a glaucoma clinic where most patients have normal acuity, the same sensitivity and specificity give a much lower PPV; the Methods template from `prisma-reporting` (`sof_table.py`) can show true and false positives per 1,000 at a clinic prevalence of the reviewer's choice.
5. *The pair was never tested.* Q97 and Q30 appear in different threshold tables and never together (ID 92 Table 5 p6). A clinic asking both is running two one-item tests at two thresholds, and the paper's pair statistics (Q97 + Q15; Q30 + Q12) are about other pairs.
6. *Not LVR need.* Neither item, nor any instrument in the review, has been compared with a clinician's judgement that the patient needs rehabilitation (G4).

**Candidate add-on items for glaucoma, ranked** (from `paper/candidate_items.md` section 3; none has item-level accuracy against any reference, so all are pilot candidates):
1. A peripheral-field mobility item: "Do you fail to see people or cars approaching you from the side?" (Sumi 2003 item 16; walking domain r = -0.61 with better-eye mean deviation in 147 glaucoma patients, ID 110 p4; GAL-9 Q6 "seeing objects come from the side" as the English alternative, no glaucoma data in the included set, ID 55 p15).
2. A night / low-light item: "Do you feel uneasy going out at night because of your visual problems?" (Sumi item 21; going-out domain r = -0.63 with mean deviation, ID 110 p4) or GAL-9 Q2 / Q4 (GAL-9 total vs contrast sensitivity rho -0.62 in maculopathy, ID 55 p15).
3. A glare item: "Walking in high glare areas" (Bibby 2007 item 18, the hardest of 35 items; instrument vs binocular field r = -0.762, n = 30, ID 120 p4, p5, p7) or APEDS-VFQ items 6-9 (ID 5 p1).
4. A global mobility item: "Do you have mobility problems because of your vision?" (GAL-9 companion question G3; positive in 18.0%, 31.0% and 71.4% across mild, moderate and severe contrast-sensitivity groups, ID 55 p13).
5. From the survey itself, Q12 "street sign at a crosswalk" (AUC 0.82 at 0.3 logMAR as decoded, probable; the only item serving both thresholds; ID 92 p4-6), with the caveat that its link to field or contrast is an inference.

Reading across the table: brief self-report items reach sensitivity 0.8-0.9 only where the reference is acuity and the sample is enriched or derivation; against field loss (0.20-0.50) and in community samples with few positives the sensitivity is lower or imprecise; specificity is the more stable property (0.7-0.9). Stalin 2025 and the NHATS self-report paper may change this picture and should be retrieved before the manuscript is finalised.

## Limitations of the synthesis (SWiM item 9)

One research group's cohort supplies every accuracy figure for the index questions; no comparison has more than one independent dataset, so no between-study heterogeneity could be examined and nothing was pooled; thresholds and reference definitions differ in every study; all accuracy reference standards are acuity-based or charted composites that cannot register field or contrast loss; no study measured need for rehabilitation; ten CIs are approximate (back-calculated); the COSMIN protocol hypotheses H1-H6 were written by the review team after the fact (`analysis/instruments/protocol_hypotheses.md`); vote counting was not used; the included set is provisional (reviewer has decided 40 of 135 records); AI assistance (Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Sonnet 5.5) drafted the groupings, tables, ratings and this text, and every number remains to be checked against its source page by the authors.
