_Converted from `LVR_Q_Study_Outline.docx`._

## Project overview

A review article for Glaucoma Today on LVR patient questionnaires.

Should cover:

- What tools exist
- How good these two questions are as screeners
- How to use them in a clinic workflow

**Objective:** to provide the evidence base for how the LVR clinic is about to screen patients.

## PICO

- **Population:** adults seen in ophthalmology or glaucoma clinics (potential LVR candidates)
- **Index test:** brief self-report screening items (2 questions: restaurant menu and keyboard)
- **Comparator:** measured visual acuity and visual field, full-length LVR questionnaires, clinician-judged LVR need
- **Outcome:** sensitivity, specificity, AUC, time to administer, feasibility in a technician work-up

## Methods

### Search strategy

A structured literature search was conducted on 4 October 2026 in PubMed (including MEDLINE) and Scopus. The search combined three concepts: (1) low vision, visual impairment and legal blindness; (2) questionnaires, self-report measures and screening questions; and (3) diagnostic accuracy, including sensitivity, specificity, receiver operating characteristic curves and area under the curve. In PubMed, Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) were combined with title and abstract keywords. In Scopus, equivalent keywords were searched in article titles, abstracts and keywords. Searches were limited to publications from 2000 onward in English or French, and studies with paediatric populations in the title were excluded.

### Record management

Records were imported into Zotero (version 7) for reference management. Records that could not be imported directly were retrieved by digital object identifier or entered manually from the database export. Duplicate records were identified using Zotero's duplicate detection, and each potential duplicate was verified manually by title, publication year and journal before merging. The search identified 166 records (PubMed, n = 78; Scopus, n = 88). After removal of 39 duplicates, 127 unique records remained for screening.

### Study selection

Titles and abstracts were screened against predefined eligibility criteria by one reviewer (MM), ? with a second reviewer independently checking a random 20% sample. Studies were included if they evaluated a self-report questionnaire or screening item against a measure of visual function or low vision rehabilitation need in adults. Potentially eligible articles (include and maybe records) underwent full-text review, and reasons for exclusion were recorded. Data extraction into a standard table: instrument, number of items, population (glaucoma %), reference standard, sensitivity / specificity / AUC, time to administer, validation method, languages.
