Abstract
Purpose – The use of drugs outside the approved indication, called off-label use, is a growing
phenomenon in medical practice. Especially, when a drug approved for systemic use is used locally in
small quantities, the drug price per treatment can fall far below the potential value of the drug. This
paper aims to outline the potential value of off-label Avastinw used for wet/neovascular age-related
macular degeneration (AMD), the leading cause of legal blindness in western countries.
Design/methodology/approach – The van Westendorp price sensitivity measurement (PSM)
method was used to find an optimal price range for an Avastin formulation potentially approved by
German regulatory authorities for neovascular/wet AMD. A survey was conducted among a majority
of German AMD specialists as the central part of the buying center. Germany, the largest
pharmaceutical market in Europe, was selected for the study.
Findings – The effective total response rate was 25.1 percent (51 questionnaires).The price range
found is clearly above the price for currently used “off-label” Avastin and far below the price for other
comparable drugs approved for the treatment of neovascular AMD. The van Westendorp PSM method
found for Avastin used in the eye the optimal price at e95 ($131), the indifference price at e200 ($276)
and the acceptable price range between e90 ($124) and e310 ($428). The discussion provides further
implications for pricing strategies for new drugs.
Originality/value – As price sensitivity and availability of drugs have become an increasingly
political and public topic, off-label application of drugs is turning into an important challenge for the
pharmaceutical industry. Taken in total, the results provide support for the view that off-label
application not only creates major threats for pharmaceutical companies, but can also offer growth
opportunities.
Keywords Pharmaceuticals industry, Drugs, Germany
Paper type Case study

Klaver et al., 1998). Multiple studies have shown that the disease has a prevalence that

among patients with AMD is the neovascular, so-called “wet” form of AMD. It is
characterized by new, pathologic vessel growth in the eye.
The use of drugs outside the approved indication, so-called off-label use, is a
growing phenomenon in medical practice. Especially, when a drug approved for
systemic use is used locally in small quantities, the drug price per treatment can fall far
below the potential value of the drug. This case study outlines the potential value of
off-label use of Avastinw for wet/neovascular AMD.
Anti-VEGF therapy
Pathologic vessel growth, so-called angiogenesis, has been a major field of interest in
cancer treatment since the early 1970s. Especially, one growth factor has been strongly
associated with angiogenesis in different forms of cancers. This factor has been termed
the “Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor” (VEGF). In the mid-1990s, the importance of
VEGF slowly became also evident for a number of ocular diseases associated with
pathologic vessel growth and increased vascular permeability (Aiello et al., 1995;
Spilsbury et al., 2000; Ozaki et al., 1997; Williams, 1998).
Anti-VEGF therapy was primarily developed to support chemotherapy in cancer
treatment. Its value in ophthalmology became apparent later. Many of the current
anti-VEGF drugs in ophthalmology are related anti-VEGF cancer drugs.
Several pharmaceutical companies have started to develop drugs to inhibit VEGF in
the eye. The first companies to obtain regulatory approval for intraocular anti-VEGF
treatment were Eyetech Pharmaceuticals (Pegaptanib/Macugenw-2004) and Genentech
Inc. (Ranibizumab/Lucentisw-2006). Both drugs bind VEGF. Lucentis was designed to
bind all subforms of VEGF, whereas Macugenw was designed to just bind the most
frequent subform VEGF-165. Even though both drugs have not been directly compared
in clinical trials, it appears evident, based on clinical trials and clinical experience, that
Macugen is only able to slow down vision loss in patients with “wet” AMD, whereas
Lucentis is able to stabilize vision in more than 90 percent of such patients at one and
two years follow-up (Chakravarthy et al., 2006; Rosenfeld et al., 2006). In addition, a
significant portion of patients is regaining vision. These are impressive results,
especially when compared to treatment options ten years ago when destructive,
thermal lasers were used to stop the progression of “wet” AMD with very poor
functional outcomes.
Lucentis is widely used for “wet” AMD in countries where it is approved and
reimbursed by health care systems.
Avastin
The worldwide first anti-VEGF drug to obtain marketing authorization was Avastin
(Bevacizumab) in early 2004. It was first approved for the treatment of widespread
cancer of the colon in combination with a chemotherapy regimen. The drug, designed
for intravenous infusion, was derived from the same precursor mouse antibody as
Lucentis. As of 2004, Avastin was used “off label” (outside the approved indication)
first as a systemic infusion and later as an injection into the eye (Michels et al., 2005;
Moshfeghi et al., 2006; Rosenfeld, Fung and Puliafito, 2005; Rosenfeld, Moshfeghi
and Puliafito, 2005). There is an increasing number of prospective clinical studies

There is strong cost awareness in Germany and overall budgets are limited
(Statistisches Bundesamt, 2006). Even though Lucentis is reimbursed, it is obvious to
most ophthalmologists that the introduction of an inexpensive new treatment will have
significant impact on future budgets and reimbursement of other ophthalmologic
services in private practice and in clinics. In addition, there is the option for
inexpensive but “off-label” Avastin. AMD specialists have used this drug “off-label”
for more than a year before Lucentis was approved and are currently using it for
VEGF-related eye diseases other than neovascular/“wet” AMD, as mentioned earlier.
The German legislature has clear guidelines for the reimbursement of off-label
drugs. Three aspects have to be fulfilled to obtain reimbursement for off-label therapy:
(1) treatment of a severe disease;
(2) no other approved therapy available; and
(3) good chance of treatment success based on current data.
With the approval of Lucentis, an effective drug for all lesion types of neovascular
AMD has become available, which should lead to a reimbursement halt for Avastin in
Germany. However, it currently becomes obvious that this legislation is not enforced.
A further important aspect is the potential risk of legal actions against AMD
specialists following an adverse event using Avastin for the treatment of neovascular
AMD. To date, no such case has been reported.
The present study addresses several areas of interest. Different from pricing finding
strategies for new drugs under patent protection used by the pharmaceutical industry,
the study uses – as commonly done for products in unregulated markets – the central
part of the buying center to find a price range for a potentially approved Avastin eye
formulation for the treatment of wet AMD in Germany.
Several aspects affect the industry when considering prices for innovative, new
drugs. On the one hand, there are clearly drivers for lower drug prices such as overall
health care costs, rising health care insurance costs, decreasing budgets for
ophthalmologists, and risk for lower reimbursement of medical professional work in
case of higher drug prices. On the other hand, very low prices for new, innovative, and
most effective drugs will clearly limit pharmaceutical industry’s interest in further
research and development of new products in that field, and will subsequently affect
future treatment options. In addition, a marketing authorization improves doctor’s
safety with regard to reimbursement and legal issues and justifies a higher drug price.
The purpose of this case study is to discuss the pricing implications for an Avastin
formulation potentially approved in Germany in comparison to other treatments for
wet AMD.
Methods
Germany was selected for the Avastin price analysis in ophthalmology for several
reasons. The German market is the largest pharmaceutical market within the
European Union and the third largest in the world. German AMD specialists, different
from their colleagues in other EU countries, had in 2007 the largest experience with all
anti-VEGF drugs. This was primarily due to the rapid adoption of Avastin as off-label
AMD treatment and the fast nation-wide reimbursement of Lucentis following its
European approval. In addition, cost-consciousness is high among German
ophthalmologists in the context of overall spending in ophthalmology being

The AMD specialists were considered as the central part of the buying center
(Figure 2), in an intermediary role among patients, pharmaceutical industry, insurance
companies, and health care authorities.
AMD specialists, familiar with AMD treatments, were specifically addressed in
order to obtain the most valuable answers and to outline the current market situation.
The selectivity for this specific subgroup of ophthalmologists clearly limits the size
and the power of the survey. The publicly available PDT/Visudynew user list (PDT,
2007), last updated January 2007, was used to contact AMD specialists in Germany.
This list has the advantage that it is the most comprehensive publicly available list of
AMD specialists belonging to different interest groups (e.g. private practice vs public
hospital). In total, 203 surveys were sent to AMD specialists.
In general, it is most difficult to obtain a precise product price using a survey since
the customer-buying center typically has difficulty naming an exact price. The
customer buying center rather buys a product within a specific price range. van
Westendorp took this aspect into account by developing the price sensitivity
measurement (PSM) method, which is a technique developed as a survey methodology
for the measurement of price perceptions (Vlosky et al., 1999). The PSM has been
adapted by researchers and has been delivering reliable research results for specific
product contexts (Allen and Maybin, 2004; Lyon, 2002; Draeger, 2000).
The van Westerdorp method allows obtaining an acceptable price range for a
certain product. A majority of customers accept a price within this range. The most
relevant findings in this type of analysis are considered to be the optimal price point
(OPP), the indifference price point (IPP) and the acceptable price range. To determine
the price sensitivity, the van Westendorp method requires answering a special set of
four questions (Allen and Maybin, 2004; Draeger, 2000). To derive a high-external
validity, the structure of the individual questions asks the respondents to qualify their
price based on an implied assumption about quality (Vlosky et al., 1999). Furthermore,
the researcher forces a response with no point of reference other than the concept and
the question (van Westendorp, 1976). The four questions, which directly ask
respondents about prices are:
(1) Up to which price would you consider the product as low priced?
(2) At what price would you say the product is expensive but worth a
consideration?

(3) At what price would you consider the product as too expensive and you would
stop buying it?
(4) Below which price would you consider the product too cheap and you would
doubt its quality?
The intersection of the cumulative frequency distribution of the questions “too
expensive” and “too cheap” is the OPP. At this point, the resistance to buy a product is
the lowest. Optimal in this sense refers to the equal trade-off in extreme sensitivities to
the price at both ends of the price spectrum. The IPP is found at the intersection of the
cumulative frequency distribution of the questions “expensive” and “low priced”.
It refers to the price point with an equal number of respondents rating the price as
either “low priced” or “expensive”. The acceptable price range of a product is found
between the crossing of the curves “too expensive” and “low priced” and “too cheap”
and “expensive”.
We integrated the van Westendorp PSM method for an Avastin formulation
potentially approved in Germany for the treatment of “wet” AMD into a larger
questionnaire.
The price for Avastin when used intraocularly is calculated based on the price of
e4.40/mg ($6/mg). The injection procedure (filling of dead space in injection needle)
requires l2.7 mg in order to inject 1.5 mg. Accordingly, the Avastin drug price for a
single intraocular treatment was set at e12 ($16.60). The most variable price of
compounding the 400 mg Avastin vials is not taken into calculation. This compares to
a Lucentis price of about e1,500 ($2,070) per treatment.
To position the price analysis in a relevant context, we additionally evaluated
baseline demographics (age and gender), the setting in which AMD patients were
treated (public hospital, private practice, and private hospital), the number of AMD
treatments performed each month, and the use of certain treatments.
Sample description
A total of 203 questionnaires were sent to AMD specialists listed in the publicly
available PDT/Visudyne user list in June 2007. In total, 51 questionnaires were
returned by August 15, 2007. The response rate was 25.1 percent.
The mean age of the responding AMD-specialist was 44.2 years (SD 8.2 years).
The majority was male (40 vs 11).
The mean number of patients with neovascular AMD treated per month per AMD
specialist was 117. Based on these data, the participating AMD specialists would
perform approximately 71,600 treatments per year. Taking into consideration a
minimum (van Leeuwen et al., 2003) and maximum (Schrader, 2006) estimated
incidence of neovascular AMD per year in Germany, the market share of each AMD
treatment, the mean number of treatments of each AMD treatment per year (Bressler,
2001; D’Amico et al., 2006; Fung et al., 2007; Gragoudas et al., 2004; Leydolt et al., 2008),
and an estimated treatment duration of two years (except Lucentis – was approved
February 2007), an estimate on the overall number of AMD treatments within one year
can be given.
The estimated range of 20,000-50,000 new wet AMD cases per year in Germany
would lead to approximately 137,000-345,000 AMD treatments per year. The total

Price analysis
The van Westendorp analysis provided the OPP, the IPP and the acceptable price
range for an Avastin formulation potentially approved for wet AMD. Figure 4 shows
the cumulative curves for each of the four questions.
The OPP is at the intersection of the cumulative frequency curves “too cheap” or
“too expensive”. The resistance “to buy” is here the lowest. The OPP is, according the
survey at e95 ($131).
The IPP refers to the price point with equal number of respondents rating the price
as either “low priced” or “expensive”. The IPP according to the survey was at e200
($276).
The upper and lower limits of the acceptable price range are found at the crossing of
the curves “too expensive” and “low priced” and “too cheap” and “expensive”. The
acceptable price range was found to be between e90 ($124) and e310 ($428).

Lucentis price are the higher level of scientific evidence and its approval for intraocular
use in wet AMD. The growing political debate on this topic in Europe and the United
States obviously indicates that these arguments are not satisfactory to everyone (Lee
and Radford, 2008; Pollack, 2005). The presented pricing strategy for an Avastin
formulation approved for intraocular use in neovascular AMD fundamentally differs
from the price evaluation strategies used by the pharmaceutical industry for newly
approved drugs under patent protection. Drug prices are most commonly based on
costs for research and development, production, marketing and sales plus two
variables: profit margin and accruals for the company’s unsuccessful drugs that never
reached the market. The pharmaceutical industry estimates the cost of bringing a new
drug to the market currently at e800 million ($1.1billion). Profit margins can be
estimated, but accruals for unsuccessful drugs are impossible to estimate. For market
leaders and blockbusters like Lucentis or Avastin (cancer treatment) these accruals are
above average.
The concept of presented price analysis is based on the finding that the market of
anti-VEGF drugs in ophthalmology is more competitive than expected. The off-label
status of a drug regarding a specific indication is a limiting factor but clearly does not
exclude a drug from the market. In this market situation, the AMD specialists is seen
as the central part of the buying center and as the mediator within the network of
patients, pharmaceutical industry, insurance companies and health care authorities.
Based on these findings the AMD specialists are addressed in the price analysis.
To find an acceptable price range from the perspective of AMD specialists, the
van Westendorp PSM method was chosen. This tool is commonly used by marketing
specialists. It aims to find an acceptable price range rather than a specific price for a
product in open, unregulated markets. The OPP for the ophthalmic Avastin
formulation, the point where the resistance to buy is the lowest, was at e95 ($131). This
is about eight times the current Avastin price when used off-label. Estimated sales in

The price range resulting from the presented study can be interpreted from different
perspectives. On the one hand, it reflects the value of such therapy from the perspective
of an AMD specialist. On the other hand, it could show the monetary value of reducing
legal costs and reimbursement risks to AMD specialists by approving Avastin for wet
AMD. But, most likely it is a price compromise taking into account interests of all parts
of the buying center.
Major disadvantages of the van Westendorp PSM method used in the survey are
according to the literature inconsistent answers to the four questions in up to
20 percent. In addition, the price focused questions could lead to too low prices (Lyon,
2002). To limit especially the underestimation of prices monadic tests asking a single
buy (yes or no) question are usually favored. However, monadic testing requires a
relatively narrow price focus and a large number of interviewees. Neither is the case in
the presented situation. The price range of comparable anti-VEGF drugs ranges from
e12 ($16.60) to e1,500 ($2,070) and the overall potentially accessible number of AMD
specialists in Germany was limited. Following the van Westendorp PSM a monadic
testing could be performed based on the findings in this survey and within a larger
international community of AMD specialists.
The study has further limitations. There is lack of precise data on the number of
new wet AMD cases in Germany per year, requiring estimates on a maximum and
minimum incidence. The number of treatments per year for each AMD drug is based
on selected studies and may not reflect daily clinical routine. Furthermore, patients lost
in follow-up and requiring treatment for more than two years are not taken into
account.
In the longer term, an approved Avastin formulation for the intraocular treatment of
wet AMD could be of advantage for all stakeholders. More patients, especially when
taking a broader, international perspective, would gain access to an effective

The pharmaceutical industry decides to pursue an approval process of Avastin

The pharmaceutical industry decides against an approval process for Avastin

an approval process against the interest of the industry. In Italy, such an
approval process has already taken place. The German Minister of Health has

approval process for ophthalmic use. This represents more or less the current
situation. The use of Avastin will depend on further trials comparing Avastin
and Lucentis, on reimbursement strategies by insurance companies and
potentially on court rulings. If the situation in Germany is similar to the USA,
Lucentis sales will slowly decrease whereas Avastin sales will increase.
Sanctions against German compounding pharmacies by the pharmaceutical
industry will have – as in the USA – little effect.

completing his residency in Ophthalmology at the University of Lübeck (Germany). His medical
retina fellowship with Professor Philip J Rosenfeld at the Bascom Palmer Eye Institute in Miami
(USA) was supported by the German Research Foundation (DFG). In 2007, he became an

University of St Gallen. He has published more than 50 papers in peer-reviewed journals mostly
on the treatment of age-related macular degeneration (AMD). Stephan Michels is the
corresponding author and can be contacted at: stephan.michels@usz.ch
Malaika Kurz-Levin, MD, is Co-head of Medical Retina at the Department of Ophthalmology
at the University of Zurich, Switzerland. She completed medical school in Zurich Switzerland and
completed her Residency in Ophthalmology at the Department of Ophthalmology at the
University of Zurich, Switzerland. She then had a two-year fellowship in Medical Retina with
Professor Alan Bird at Moorfields Hospital in London (UK). She subsequently became Faculty
member at the Department of Ophthalmology at the University of Zurich and was Head of
Medical Retina, a position shared since 2007 with PD Dr Michels. Her research focus is on AMD
and diabetic retinopathy.
Christian Schmitz is an Assistant Professor of Marketing at the Institute of Marketing,

Administration at the University of Duisburg (Germany), at the Catholic University of Eichstaett
(Germany) and the European Business School London (UK). His doctoral thesis examined
working relationships among manufacturers and international distributors. Recent research
