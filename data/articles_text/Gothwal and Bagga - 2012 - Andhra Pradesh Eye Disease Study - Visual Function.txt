ABSTRACT
Purpose: Previous Rasch analysis of the Andhra Pradesh Eye Disease Study-Visual Function Questionnaire
(APEDS-VFQ) lacked comprehensiveness, specifically, dimensionality (whether it measures single/multiple
constructs). Therefore, using the Rasch model this study provides a detailed assessment of psychometric
properties of the APEDS-VFQ.
Methods: A total of 351 visually impaired adults (mean age, 43.3 years) were verbally administered the APEDS VFQ. Rasch analysis was used to assess the psychometric properties of the instrument.
Results: Participants could distinguish only three categories of difficulty, so response categories were reduced
from five to three. A single item (“reading small prints in newspaper/magazines”, infit mean square 1.54)
misfit the model. The overall pattern of fit statistics for item and person measures suggested that the underlying construct (visual ability) is not unidimensional. When the items were grouped into subsets based on
functional requirements (resolution, contrast sensitivity, illumination and peripheral vision) and separate
person measures were estimated for each of these domains, the first principal component contained the visual
ability and accounted for 72% of the variance. Item measure distributions could be divided into 18 strata, and
item-separation reliability was 0.99. Person measures could be divided into three statistically distinct strata
and the person-separation reliability was 0.81.
Conclusions: The APEDS-VFQ is a precise measure of visual ability in visually impaired adults in India. Similar
to other visual function questionnaires developed for the Western population, our results demonstrate that
visual ability is a two-factor composite latent variable; one dimension heavily influences reading and the other
most heavily influences peripheral vision (mobility).
KEYWORDS: Rasch analysis, Andhra Pradesh Eye Disease Study-Visual Function Questionnaire,
Psychometric properties, India

Unidimensionality constitutes an important
attribute of a good measure.3 This property is an
important prerequisite to generate a valid total score
for an instrument and the user can be certain that
a single underlying construct is being measured.5–7
Although previous Rasch analysis of the APEDS-VFQ
used fit statistics to investigate dimensionality, this
constitutes a limited aspect of the assessment. Recent
studies have indicated that fit statistics alone are an
insufficient indicator of unidimensionality; a thorough assessment using principal components analysis (PCA) of residuals is required.8 DIF is another
important related property of a good measure as its
presence violates the assumption of unidimensionality.9 DIF occurs when an item performs differently
for one subgroup compared to another, despite being
equal on the amount of the construct in both subgroups.10 Thus, DIF analysis can help to determine if
items are appropriate for use in various subgroups in
a heterogeneous sample.
Using some of the widely used VFQs such as the
Activities of Daily Vision Scale, National Eye Institute
Visual Function Questionnaire, Visual Functioning
Index-14, Visual Activities Questionnaire and the
Activity Inventory, Massof demonstrated that visual
ability is a composite variable with two factors; one
most heavily influences reading function and the other
most heavily influences mobility function.11–14 Given this
finding, the aim of the present study was to examine
whether the underlying construct (visual ability) of the
APEDS-VFQ exhibits a similar pattern. A secondary aim
was to investigate the behavior of the response categories and in case of deficiencies, to propose a revised
rating scale. Finally, Excel sheets that convert raw to
Rasch-scaled scores for those who wish to use the benefits of interval-level measurement are provided.

Subjects
Participants were visually impaired adults referred
to the Vision Rehabilitation Centres, LV Prasad Eye
Institute, India. The APEDS-VFQ was administered by
trained interviewers in a face-to-face interview to each
participant prior to any examination.
Participants were aged 18 years or older, had visual

was obtained from the LV Prasad Eye Institute Ethics

tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.
Sociodemographic data was extracted from clinical
records. Participant characteristics of those who provided

Rasch Analysis
The matrix of responses from 351 participants was subjected to Rasch analysis using the Andrich rating scale
model for polytomous data.17 The theory behind the
Rasch measurement model has been described elegantly
by Massof.18,19 Rasch analysis is an iterative procedure
that estimates interval measurement from ordinal data
and the unit is logits (log-odd units).19
Winsteps software20 (version 3.68) was used to conduct the Rasch analysis, and estimates of person ability
and item difficulties were obtained. There are a series of
components to Rasch analysis that have been described
elsewhere in detail.21,22 Briefly, we assessed the properties as described below.
Unidimensionality was assessed by examining the
item fit statistic, infit mean square (MnSq), which has
an expected value of 1.0 and is preferable for assessing
item fit as it is less sensitive to sample size compared
to standardized z values.24 We used infit MnSq values
between 0.7 and 1.3 to indicate item fit.21 Items with
MnSq values less than 0.7 suggest presence of redundant
items and values higher than 1.3 indicate unusual and/or

inappropriate response patterns, and signal misfit. While
some researchers believe that misfitting items should be
deleted to optimize the instrument’s properties, we did
not pursue this instantaneously, as we believe that item
misfit per se should not be a cause for removing items.
Furthermore an instrument is considered to have acceptable unidimensionality when at least 95% of the items
fit the Rasch measurement model.25 The presence of
misfitting items should rather alert the researcher that
there might be a problem requiring further investigation
(such as DIF analysis – described later). Item misfit can
be sample-dependent, so we investigated fit statistics
for person measures, at times removing grossly misfitting persons (standardized infit, z >2.5) who distorted
the measurement system which could potentially fix the
item misfits. Some of the reasons for person misfit could
be carelessness, fatigue, boredom or disinterest and this
could threaten the instrument’s validity. Closer analysis
of these response patterns is required before a sound
rationale can be established for removing all misfitting
persons. However, it is generally accepted that 2.5% of the
sample, by chance, may not demonstrate acceptable fit.12
Subsequently, we investigated the person measure fit
statistics and identified the outliers. Then we grouped
participants by their fit statistics to test the hypothesis that misfitting persons represent distortion by
identifiable confounding variables in the sample. We
expected two possible outcomes: (1) non-uniform DIF
(ie members of one group are favored up to a level on
the ability scale, and from that point on, the relationship
is reversed26) will vary across infit groups (steepest item
characteristic curves for small MnSq values and shallowest item characteristic curves for large MnSq values);
and/or (2) uniform DIF (when a group performs better
than another group on all ability levels26) will occur for
groups of misfitting persons. The items that showed the
most MnSq-dependent non-uniform and/or uniform
DIF would be considered the strongest candidates for
being edited from the instrument because they are sensitive to contamination by confounding variables in the
person sample. Furthermore, the presence of DIF suggests that the item is measuring an additional construct,
or dimension that may not be relevant to the intended
construct. DIF was considered notable if >1.0 logits.21
In addition to DIF, we also investigated differential
person functioning that occurs when a person may
function differently on two groups of items after controlling for an overall measure.27 For this analysis, we
divided the 16 items into 4 groups based on functional
requirements: (1) those dependent on resolution; (2)
those dependent on illumination; (3) those dependent on
contrast sensitivity; and (4) those dependent on peripheral vision. Subsequently, we estimated the person
measures from each subset of items (with item measures
and structure calibrations anchored to their originally
estimated values) and then performed exploratory factor
analysis (using varimax rotation) on the set of person
measures plus logMAR acuity, restricting the analysis

Principal components analysis of the item measure
residuals was undertaken to further assess the dimensionality of the instrument. We used the following two
criteria to establish unidimensionality: (1) a cut-off variance of 60% accounted for by the first factor (ie dimension); and (2) the eigenvalue of the first contrast should
be smaller than 3.0.8
Although previous Rasch analysis of the APEDS-VFQ
suggested shortening of the rating scale by combining
the two end categories, the resultant new category was,
however, not labeled. Furthermore, the rater use of the
revised categories in a new sample has not been evaluated. Thus, we investigated the behavior of the original
rating scale in our population. Specifically, we assessed
the response format for location of the thresholds using
category probability curves (CPC). The threshold is the
point of intersection between two adjacent categories
where the response to either of the two adjacent categories is equally probable.23 The thresholds are expected
to increase monotonically. For a well-fitting model, one
would expect an ordered set of response thresholds (first
followed by second threshold, and so on) represented by a
range of hills arranged sequentially in the CPC. However,
if threshold disordering (for example, the third threshold
is located between the first and second) occurs, then categories need to be combined to repair the disordering.
Reliability of the estimated measures was determined
by examination of the person and item separation indices
and their related separation reliability. Separation (G) is
defined as the ratio of the true spread of the measures
with their measurement error, and refers to the number
of distinct strata that can be identified in the distribution of participants and items.23,25 Consequently the item
separation index provides an estimate (in standard error,
SE, units) of the spread or separation of items along the
measurement construct, and the person separation index
gives an estimate of the spread or separation of participants along the measurement construct. A separation of
2.0 is considered good and enables the distinction of three
groups or strata (number of distinct strata = (4G+1)/3])23
Separation, in turn, determines reliability (item separation and person separation reliability) which indicates the
degree of confidence that can be placed in the consistency
of the estimates (range 0–1; coefficients equivalent to or
exceeding 0.80 are considered good which corresponds to
a separation index of 2.0).25 However, person separation
reliability depends on both the average SE of the person
measure estimate and the standard deviation (SD) of
the person measure distribution. Hence, separation reliability is sample-dependent. Given this, we used item
separation reliability as an indicator of the instrument’s
reliability instead of person separation reliability.
To assess the ability of the instrument to appropriately target the population under consideration, we
visually inspected the Wright person-item map. A well targeted instrument should include a set of items that

span the full range of person estimates. Targeting and
reliability (or measurement precision) are related concepts. Reliability is not uniform across the entire range
of test scores. Scores at the edges of the test’s range, for
example, generally have more error associated with
them than scores closer to the middle of the range.
Therefore, a Fisher information function is a better
method to illustrate targeting and this function defines
the upper bound of measurement precision expected by
the Rasch model given the observed responses.19,28 The
Fisher information function is an index related to the
distribution of item measures but incorporates SE of the
estimate for each item. In a well-targeted instrument this
would indicate the highest level of information at the
mean of the person measures. The Fisher information
function was superimposed on the person-item map.
Factor analyses were performed using SPSS software
(version 15.0, Chicago, IL). A P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS
Assessment of Response Categories
Figure 1a illustrates the CPC of an item in its original
5-category format showing the range of visual function
for which each of the five response options were most
likely to be chosen. The CPC plots the probability of
choosing a given category (on the y-axis) for every place
along the measured construct (visual function on the
x-axis). Useful categories are those with high probabilities
that span a distinct portion of the construct. However,
the figure shows that categories 1 (“little difficulty”) and
3 (“great deal of difficulty, but can still manage”) do not
have a range along the scale where they are the most
likely categories to be selected and disordering of thresholds is observed. Disordering is also shown by the lack of
monotonic increase of the thresholds (from category 1 to
2 and 3 to 4) in Table 3.
A couple of rescoring options were tested, however, a
global rescore was found to be the most appropriate solution. All disordered thresholds were resolved (Figure 1b
and Table 4) by rescoring that involved reducing the original 5-category scale (0, 1, 2, 3, 4) to a 3-category scale (0,
1, 1, 1, 2) by merging the three middle categories (“a little
difficulty”, “moderate difficulty”, and “great deal of difficulty but can still manage”). The resultant three categories
included: 0 “no difficulty”, 1 “can manage with difficulty”,
and 2 “cannot manage”. In essence, the end categories
were untouched and the intermediate categories were
combined in the revised rating scale of the APEDS-VFQ.

DISCUSSION
The results of the present study are in accordance with
similar such studies using other VFQs which concluded
that visual ability is a composite variable and can be
segregated into two independent dimensions.11–14 It is
© 2012 Informa Healthcare USA, Inc.

probably the choice of the items in a given VFQ that
can bias the estimates of visual ability toward one or
the other dimension.
However, unidimensionality is one of the requirements of the Rasch model and needs to be satisfied in
case items are summed to generate a valid summary
score. Despite the finding from the present study of
the presence of two dimensions in visual ability, we
believe that as is the case with other most widely used
VFQs,12,14 the APEDS-VFQ also functions as if it were
unidimensional and, therefore, should be considered as
a composite variable. In the present study, reading and
contrast sensitivity items loaded heavily on one factor,
and peripheral vision and items dependent on illumination loaded on another. Reading and contrast sensitivity,
and peripheral vision and illumination are not measures
of independent factors; rather they are measures that
incorporate both factors. One dimension most heavily

influences responses to items that describe reading and

resolution. This dimension is related to seeing detail that
is disrupted by loss of visual acuity. The other dimension
most heavily influences responses to items that describe
peripheral vision and illumination. This dimension is
related to size of the seeing area and level of lighting that
is disrupted by contrast sensitivity and visual field loss.
These two factors together constitute the visual ability
variable and explain 72% of the response variance. The
low correlation between visual acuity and person measures further supports the two dimensions in visual ability. The two factors mentioned above probably describe
the properties of the participant’s visual ability rather
than the visual impairment. Furthermore, the functional
difficulties of low vision patients are varied and the
items included in a VFQ must be as comprehensive as
possible so as to include the entire range of activities.
In the case of the APEDS-VFQ used in the present
study, we found a single most-misfitting item (“reading
small prints in the newspaper/magazines”). The large
MnSq value for this item suggests that there is high variability in the sample in the functional requirements of the
activity. We speculate that such high variability is perhaps related to the double-barreled nature of this item,
in that it relates to reading small prints in the newspaper
and/or magazines; print size and contrast vary among
newspapers and across newspapers and magazines.
Consequently, the visual demand could be expected to be
different for reading newspapers versus magazines. For
example, a participant may have had “mild difficulty”
with reading small prints in newspapers but may have

had a “great deal of difficulty” with reading such print
in magazines. Despite this the participant was required
to respond to this item as though it is a single activity.
Normally, researchers would delete such a misfitting
item so as to optimize the measurement properties of an
instrument,13,29,30 but we did not pursue this. Care needs
to be exercised while identifying misfitting items, as the
ability of a single fit statistic to correctly identify misfit
across a range of assessment scenarios and instruments
has been questioned in the Rasch literature.31 It is therefore suggested that the removal of these items should be
assessed against impact on clinical utility.29 Although the
impact was not assessed in the present study (as it was
not the intent of this study), our decision to retain the
single most-misfitting item appears logical given that
it improves content validity as “reading small prints in
newspapers, books or magazines” is among the chief
presenting complaints of people to a low vision clinic.
This is evidenced by the fact that the item was applicable
to 94% of our participants. Furthermore, there was no
substantial worsening or improvement of the item or
person separation reliability indices, further supporting
our decision to retain this item.
Rather than delete misfitting items, their presence
should suggest the existence of a problem which requires
further investigation (such as DIF analysis). Furthermore,
item misfit can be sample-dependent, so we investigated
fit statistics for the person measures. Sometimes removing grossly misfitting persons could fix the item misfits.
However, the number of most-misfitting persons was
probably too small to alter the item fit statistics and the
single item continued to misfit. In our DIF analysis, we
found the same most-misfitting item to demonstrate
notable DIF by visual acuity suggesting again that it is a
potential candidate for deletion, as the presence of DIF
violates one of the requirements of unidimensionality of
the model. Nonetheless, the existence of DIF in itself is
not an indication of a problem with the item. However,
just as judgment is required to determine cut-offs to flag
DIF items, judgment is required to determine if the item
is essential to the measurement process. Instruments are
often a mixture of items that don’t measure the same
thing in the same way. Items that exhibit performance
differences on the rest of the instrument will be flagged
for DIF but may be essential to the construct of interest.
Furthermore, DIF may occur when an item measures
dimensions other than the dimension measured by the
other items. In the case of the APEDS-VFQ, examination
of the items and the pattern of DIF indicated that slight
multidimensionality was due to two dimensions of interest to the researcher (as mentioned earlier).
Dimensionality per se is a bad property of an instrument if the construct of interest is a composite variable
(such as the visual ability variable in most VFQs including the APEDS-VFQ). In contrast, if the dimensionality
represents subgroups of participants that behave differently, then the construct is not a composite, but rather
is confounded, which is undesirable. Given that the

Among the desirable qualities of a good measure is
adequate reliability. The high item-separation reliability
found for the APEDS-VFQ in the present and earlier
study suggest that the items on the APEDS-VFQ are
well separated relative to errors of their location on
the scale, thereby defining a clear scale and that great
confidence can be placed in the replicability of the item
placement across future samples. Furthermore, it also
suggests a high probability that items with high difficulty estimates are really more difficult than items with
lower difficulty estimates.32 Thus, it would be safe to
conclude that the APEDS-VFQ is a precise measure of
visual ability in the visually impaired in India. Similar
high item-separation reliability has been reported for
the Activity Inventory developed by Massof.12,13
A well-targeted instrument is a logical prerequisite
when measurement and evaluation of improvements in
patient outcomes are desired. In the present study, items
mostly targeted participants with lower visual ability
and gaps remained for those with relatively higher
visual ability. More items (difficult) may be needed at
the lower end of the scale to distinguish among levels
of those with higher visual ability.
Inspection of the rating scale format for the APEDS VFQ revealed issues regarding the ordering of response
categories indicating that the response scale does not
work as intended. Disordering of thresholds was identified but a reduction in number of response categories from five to three resolved any disordering. In the
earlier Rasch analysis of the APEDS-VFQ, thresholds
were also disordered and combining two middle categories (“moderate difficulty” and “great deal of difficulty but can still manage”) was suggested. However,
in the present study there was a need to combine all
three middle categories (including “little difficulty”),
indicating that participants were unable to reliably
distinguish between what is meant by the terms “a
little”, “moderate” and “a great deal”, which could be
replaced by a single category (ie “some difficulty”) for
optimal functioning of the response categories. Despite
the reduction in the response options from five to three,
the revised APEDS-VFQ scale showed good item and
person-separation reliability supporting this revision.
Nonetheless, the new rating scale needs to be validated
in future studies prior to being recommended for use.
This study builds on previous Rasch analysis of
the APEDS-VFQ. In the present study, we specifically
assessed the dimensionality of the instrument. Like other
VFQs, we found the latent variable (visual ability) of the
APEDS-VFQ to be a two-dimension composite variable.
