ABSTRACT

ARTICLE HISTORY

Purpose: To characterize the quality of life and mental health status of patients with uveitis and
investigate predictors of psychological problems.
Methods: A total of 245 patients and 105 controls were enrolled in this cross-sectional study.
Quality of life, psychological status, socio-demographic and clinical data were obtained from
questionnaires and medical records. Multivariate regression analyses and Receiver Operating
Characteristic (ROC) were applied to obtain the model predicting psychological problems of
patients.
Results: Of 245 patients, 16.7% and 26.5% (P < .0001) screened positive for anxiety and depression,
respectively. The model predicting anxiety was comprised of low annual household income and poor
self-reported visual function (P = .029, P < .0001, respectively), with an AUC of ROC of 0.744. The model
predicting depression was comprised of poor self-reported visual function and ocular complications
(P < .0001, P = .012, respectively), with an AUC of 0.78.
Conclusions: Economic hardship, ocular complications, and poor self-reported visual function are predictors of mental problems in patients with uveitis.

Uveitis is a potentially blinding inflammatory disease causing
5–10% of visual impairment worldwide1 with up to 35% of
patients with uveitis suffering from severe vision loss, including legal blindness.2,3 The prevalence of uveitis in the Chinese
population was about 1.52‰, the blind rate is about 0.86‰,4
and is on the rise.5 As an inflammatory disease, uveitis can
also lead to psychological problems as well,2,6,7 such as anxiety
and depression.8,9
Based on the bio-psycho-social medical model, the connections between psychological, physical, and social factors
must be considered when assessing their influence on
patients. For example, more negative socio-demographic
characteristics may increase the occurrence of psychological
problems10,11 and clinical features, like poor visual function,
are also risk factors for psychological problems.12 Studies have
shown psychological problems can affect patient treatment
compliance and ultimately affect the efficacy of
treatment.13,14 Early detection of and timely intervention for
psychological problems may prevent the occurrence of
adverse events and improve patient prognosis. In addition,
prediction models for psychological problems may provide
clinicians with some guidance in terms of drug selection,
surgical timing, disease education, and clinical care, as well

With the help of self-administered questionnaires, patients
with retinitis pigmentosa,15 macular degeneration,16and newly

depressive symptoms and poor self-reported health and
vision. At present, while there have been studies abroad
focusing on the mental health and quality of life of patients
with uveitis,19–21 there has been almost no domestic research.
The purpose of this study was to assess the mental health
status and vision- and health-related quality of life of patients
with uveitis in China and identify risk factors related to
mental health. To our knowledge, this study is the first to
focus on predictors of psychological problems in Chinese
patients with uveitis.

Quality of Life and Mental Health

Design and Procedures
To ensure the reliability and professionalism of diagnoses and
avoid any possible influence on the reliability of the experimental data due to patient doubt, uveitis specialists with
medical qualifications were required to provide treatment to
the patients.
Before data collection, the purpose of the study and ethical questions was clearly explained to the subjects and questions related to the study were answered. After obtaining
trust and informed consent from the subjects, the collection
of relevant data formally began. Subjects were asked to
complete three self-administered questionnaires related to
the quality of life (QOL) and mental health, as well as
a questionnaire on their demographic characteristics by
themselves unless they were illiterate or unable to do so
due to poor vision. In these cases, a trained interviewer
read and completed the questionnaires based on patient
answers. After completion of the questionnaires, the patients
were given a physical ophthalmic examination and the
necessary clinical and auxiliary examinations required by
the doctor according to their own conditions.
Socio-Demographics and Clinical Characteristics
The socio-demographic characteristics of the subjects collected with the questionnaires included gender, age, nationality, education, occupation, and annual household income.
The clinical characteristics of the patients were collected
from medical records and included laterality of disease,
current best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), duration of
disease, course of disease (acute, recurrent, or chronic),
diagnosis of diseases, anatomic location of inflammation,
uveitis-associated systemic inflammatory diseases, current
activity of disease, current and previous treatments, history

through
self-administered

including

Visual Function Questionnaire (CHI-VFQ-25), a Chinese version of the 36-item Short Form Health Survey (CHI-SF-36),
and a Chinese version of the Hospital Anxiety and Depression
Scale (CHI-HADS). The cutoff values for the HADS-Anxiety
(HADS-A) and HADS-Depression (HADS-D), which were
used to evaluate the positive tendencies for anxiety and
depression in the subjects, were set as 8. Please see supplementary information for a detailed description of the
questionnaires.
Statistical Analyses
Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS statistical software (SPSS Inc, version 14.0, Chicago, Illinois). Wilcoxon
rank-sum tests were conducted to compare the scores of self administered questionnaires between patients and controls.
The Chi-square test was conducted to determine differences
in categorical variables between the anxious and not anxious
groups, as well as between the depressed and not depressed
groups. Continuous variables were analyzed using Wilcoxon
rank-sum tests or Student’s t-tests. Associations between
HADS-A or HADS-D scores and VFQ-25 composite scores
were measured using Spearman correlation coefficient.
Univariate analyses were conducted to compare sociodemographic and clinical characteristics between patients with and
without a positive screen for anxiety or depression. All tests
described above were two-sided and P-value less than 0.05
was set as the significant level. A forward stepwise multivariate regression model was used to determine significant
predictors for anxiety and depression with an entry P-value

Results
QOL and Mental Health Status of Uveitis Patients
A total of 260 patients and 115 reference subjects with similar
age, sex, income, and education distributions were asked to
participate in this study. Of these, 245 patients (94.23%) and
105 reference subjects (91.3%) completely answered the questionnaires. Scores from the patient and reference groups for
the three scales are presented in Supplementary Table S1. The
patients with uveitis scored significantly lower on the CHIVFQ-25 and CHI-SF-36 for nearly all dimensions. For the
HADS-D subscale, 65 (26.5%) of the patients with uveitis had
scores of at least 8 compared to the only 10 (9.5%) in the
reference group, and the patients with uveitis scored significantly higher than the reference group (P < .0001).

patients were considered “depressed,” while 180 (73.5%)
patients were considered “not depressed” (Table 1).

Univariate Analysis
A correlation matrix involving all variables predicting positive-HADA and positive-HADD was developed using univariate analysis (Table 3). Patients who discontinued work due to
uveitis had a greater probability of being anxious or depressed
(P = .006 and P = .057, respectively). By contrast, a higher
education level or annual household income was protective
against anxiety (P = .037 and P = .006, respectively) and
depression (P = .002 and P = .007, respectively). In terms of
the clinical traits, patients with worse logMAR vision in either
the better and worse-seeing eye were significantly more likely
to be anxious and depressed. Ocular complication was also
a risk factor for depression (OR = 2.786, 95% CI 1.555–4.991,
P = .001), among which complicated cataracts (P = .023),
secondary glaucoma (P = .014), and macular edema
(P = .041) significantly increased the incidence of depression.
There was also a significant correlation between secondary
glaucoma and anxiety (P = .016). In addition, patients who
were current biologic agent users, IMT recipients, ever changed IMT regimens, or treated with frequent intraocular or
periocular injections were significantly more likely to be
depressed (P = .059, P = .068, P = .039, and P = .003, respectively). Notably, a higher VFQ composite score appeared
protective against anxiety (OR = 0.954, 95% CI 0.953–0.975,
P < .0001) and depression (OR = 0.94 95% CI 0.92–0.961,
P < .0001).
Multivariate Analysis
Based on univariate analysis (Table 3), models were obtained by
forward stepwise multiple regression analysis (Table 4). A higher
annual household income or VFQ-25 composite score was

protective against anxiety, while a poor BCVA in the better seeing eye, discontinuing work due to uveitis, and uveitis associated secondary glaucoma significantly increased the risk
of anxiety. Current treatment with systemic biological agents, the
presence of ocular complications, such as secondary glaucoma
and complicated cataracts, and receiving frequent periocular or
intraocular injections significantly increased the risk of depression. Higher socio-demographic characteristics, such as a higher
educational level or annual household income, and higher VFQ25 composite score were protective against depression. As shown
in the Model HADS-A 3 and Model HADS-D 3, the VFQ-25
composite score was more predictive of anxiety and depression
than BCVA, as the final models (Model HADS-A 3) comprised
of the annual household income and VFQ-25 composite score
explained nearly 74.4% of the variation in the HADS-A
(OR = 0.398, 95% CI 0.174–0.911, P = .029 and OR = 0.958,
95% CI 0.938–0.978, P < .0001, respectively). The final model
(Model HADS-D 3) comprised of ocular comorbidities and
VFQ-25 composite score explained nearly 78.0% of the variation
in HADS-D (OR = 2.259, 95% CI 1.2–4.252, P = .012 and
OR = 0.943 95% CI 0.922–0.964, P < .0001, respectively).
These values were much higher than those derived from other
models (Figure 2).

Discussion
Compared with the control group, the QOL and mental
health of patients with uveitis were significantly worse. Poor
socio-demographic status, ocular complications, and poor
self-reported visual function were predictors of mental health
in patients with uveitis. VFQ-25 composite score was a more
prominent predictor of anxiety and depression than BCVA.
The best model consisted of low annual household income
and poor self-reported visual function and accounted for
74.4% of the variation in HADS-A. The best model consisted
of poor self-reported visual function and ocular complications
and accounted for 78.0% of the variation in HADS-D. Patients
with uveitis and psychological distress identified by these
predictive indicators and models need to be included in
routine uveitis clinical evaluations, as well as routine psychological clinical evaluations, to prevent adverse events and
improve patient treatment compliance. This study emphasizes
the need for attention toward the psychological problems and
disease education of patients and suggests relevant departments to promote the improvement of the medical security
system for patients with uveitis.
In this study, 26.5% of the uveitis group screened positive
for depression based on the HADS, which is significantly
higher than 9.5% in the reference group. This result is consistent with previous reports.2,6 However, the incidence of
depression in our patients was relatively low, which may be
affected by different diseases and populations. Reasonable
explanations for the high incidence of depression in patients
with uveitis are the presence of chronic inflammation and
cytokine dysregulation.26,27 When the body experiences peripheral inflammation, the innate immune system produces
inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin (IL)-1α, IL-1β,
IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor-α, that can cross the blood–
brain barrier and cause disease-associated issues, including

Lower education levels, discontinuing work due to uveitis,
and lower household income are risk factors for anxiety and
depression. These results are consistent with a previous study
showing psychological distress in old age caused by financial
hardship in childhood may be reduced by education and
employment later in life.30 The protective mechanism of
higher education may involve personal characteristics associated with varying levels of stress tolerance and different
occupational restrictions, which may be related to stress exposure levels and physical health.31 Discontinuing work adds to
the economic burden of individuals and causes mental illness.
Depression and suicidal behavior accompany economic crises
in low-income and middle-income countries, while some
affluent countries offer provisions to help the unemployed
escape adverse consequences.32 However, the reimbursement

ratio of the medical insurance of these patients needing long term clinical care is relatively low in China.33 Our results
remind clinicians to consider the patient’s financial situation
when considering treatment. In addition, authorities need to
improve the medical security system for patients with chronic
inflammatory diseases that require long-term or even lifelong
medication.

use of biologic agents sometimes aggravates the economic and
mental burden of patients. Furthermore, biologic agents are
usually used in patients with severe disease who have failed to
respond to systemic hormone therapy and IMT. In this context, the addition of biological agents may mean an increased
difficulty in curing the disease, resulting in increased psychological burden. Also, the majority of patients treated with
biologic agents had poor visual function and there is a clear
relationship between poor visual function and depression.
Moreover, the application of biologic agents in uveitis is not
common in China and patients lack awareness of and receptivity to these new drugs.
No correlation was found between current oral corticosteroid therapy or IMT and depression, yet studies have shown
IMT can be beneficial to mental health.36 Notably, univariate
analysis showed a history of changing IMT was predictive of
depression, which is consistent with findings by Ying Qian
et al.,37 perhaps indicating that patients with more complex
and intractable diseases have a higher risk of depression.
However, this factor is no longer significant following multivariate regression analysis. The median duration (IQR) of
diseases of all the patients was 23 (6–52.2) months, no statistically significant correlation between the duration of disease
(P = .729) and depression was found. There was also no
significant correlation between disease activity and depression
(P = .564). A possible explanation is that a cross-sectional
study can have difficulties in consistency of dynamic disease
capture.
This research indicates secondary glaucoma associated
with uveitis is a risk factor for anxiety and depression.
A large sample of 4,439,518 patients revealed a statistically
significant correlation between glaucoma and anxiety and
depression with an adjusted ORs of 10.6 (95%CI 10.0–11.0)
and 12.3 (95% CI 11.8–12.9), respectively.17 Our study also
linked complicated cataracts and depression. In the elderly,
the correlation between cataract-related visual impairment
and depression was significantly independent of demographics, life exposure, and current vision.38–40 After surgery,
the VRQOL of elderly patients significantly improves, which
is followed with a decrease in depression and cognitive
impairment.41 This may aid clinicians in determining the
timing of surgery. However, this conclusion cannot be easily
extended to patients with uveitis complicated with cataract,
because the average age of patients with uveitis complicated
with cataract is different from that of patients with cataract,
there were age-related difference in cognition between the two
groups. Univariate logistic regression analysis found that there
was a certain correlation between macular edema and depression (P = .041), while the significance was lost in the multivariate model. The incidence of uveitic macular edema
(10.2%) in this study was lower than that in another study,42
possibly because this was a cross-sectional study, a certain
number of patients were in recovery. Therefore, the present
study was of limitation in suggesting a correlation between
macular edema and depression. Research has shown that
diabetic macular edema was not associated with depressive
and anxious symptoms.43 To our knowledge, there have been

no relevant studies focusing on anxiety and depression in
patients with these three complications of uveitis. Further
prospective longitudinal research on the mental health status
of these groups may provide a new theoretical basis for the
selection of surgical timing and rational treatment.
This study found the risk of depression increased as the
number of periocular or intraocular injections increased.
There are a few possible explanations. Patients’ confidence
declined as the number of injections increased. In fact, most
patients who were treated more frequently were also those
experiencing frequent relapses. Additionally, frequent local
injection therapy increases the incidence of local complications. Previous study has shown that patients being treated
with systemic anti-inflammatory therapy did not have significant systemic side effects, while patients treated with fluocinolone acetonide intraocular implants had a higher incidence
of cataract and secondary glaucoma.44 Our results suggest
clinicians should reasonably choose systematic or local treatments based on the psychological status of patients with
uveitis.
Studies have linked visual impairment to mental illness.45
Our study also showed that poor BCVA in the better-seeing
eye was a risk factor for positive-HADA, which is consistent
with previous research results.9 This is possibly because
BCVA in the better-seeing eye determines a person’s visual
potential. However, Spearman correlation analysis identified
a mild or moderate correlation between VFQ composite score
and HADS-A and HADS-D score, possibly because this was
a cross-sectional study involving a non-single disease with
patients at different stages of treatment. The previous study
has found a significant correlation between BCVA and
VRQOL.46 This study attempted to explore which of the
above two factors has a stronger role in indicating the risk
of anxiety and depression. Multiple regression analysis
revealed the VFQ-25 composite score was a more prominent
predictor of positive-HADA and positive-HADD than
BCVA, and the AUC of the ROC of the final regression
model notably increased to 0.744 and 0.780, respectively,
when the VFQ-25 composite score was considered.
A similar phenomenon has been observed in patients with
ocular inflammatory disease.37 Visual function was not only
reflected in vision but also in contrast sensitivity and visual
field, which were also associated with mental problems.18
Furthermore, patient’s visual functioning is important in all
aspects of life, including activities near and far, self-care,
social contact, and mental health. Thus, it is not realistic to
discuss visual function separately from specific life situations. VFQ-25, containing 12 dimensions, can more comprehensively evaluate patient visual function and VRQOL
than BCVA. Depressed patients scored lower in almost all
dimensions of the VFQ-25 than non-depressed patients.
Therefore, BCVA is not a sufficient indicator with which to
assess patient vision and the contribution of VRQOL scales,
like VFQ-25, is indispensable.
Based on the above research results, it is of great significance to evaluate the QOL of patients reasonably. Apart from
the indirect method of questionnaires, the direct methods also

play an important role in QOL assessment, because they are
more sensitive and comprehensive. Time trade-off (TTO) and
standard gamble (SG), commonly used direct methods, allow
QOL to be compared among different health conditions without the need for mapping.47,48 For example, by comparing the
TTO utility value, researchers found that the HRQOL in
patients with uveitis with poor vision was worse than that of
patients with AIDS.48,49 However, TTO and SG presented
cognitive bias for some patients,50 and sometimes, the delivery
of questions related to death may cause discomfort and panic
in subjects. In contrast, questionnaires are generally easier to
answer, and patients may be more willing to cooperate.48
Therefore, the indirect and direct methods have their own
advantages and disadvantages, and rational development and
application of the above two methods will help to objectively
evaluate the QOL of patients.
Based on our results, the psychological status of patients with
uveitis should be concerned. Several studies have shown that
anxiety and depression can affect the treatment compliance of
patients with some chronic diseases, thus affecting treatment
efficacy.51 Wantanee Sittivarakul attributed poor understanding
of disease by patients with ocular inflammatory disease to the
cognitive impairment complicated by anxiety and depression.52
A similar association has been found in patients with glaucoma.53
Due to the higher tendency for depression, clinicians should pay
more attention to the disease education of patients with uveitis. In
addition, early detection and timely intervention in the mental
distress of patients with uveitis may be of great significance in
restoring cognition and improving patient compliance.
There are several limitations in our study. First, the enrolled
patients are not representative of all patients with uveitis in
China, so the results may not be generalizable to other populations. Second, while the HADS scale used in the study has been
verified to have good sensitivity and specificity at the threshold
of 8, it is still not a formal psychiatric evaluation for the diagnosis of anxiety and depression. Third, since our study was
a cross-sectional study, a significant number of our patients
were in the disease stabilization and recovery stage, so visual
dysfunction and symptoms of anxiety and depression may be
lower in our patient population than in other clinical studies. In
addition, considering systemic corticosteroid and immunomodulatory treatment, dosage and duration of administration were
not considered as stratified variables, which may inhibit assessing
the relationships between systemic therapy and anxiety and
depression. Therefore, longitudinal studies to determine differences in QOL and psychological status of patients at different
stages of treatment are important avenues of future study.
In this study, we evaluated the impact of uveitis on the
QOL and mental health of patients. Low socio-demographic
characteristics, ocular complications, and poor self-reported
visual function were identified as important factors when
predicting the mental health of patients with uveitis.
Compared with the BCVA, self-reported visual function was
a better predictor of mental problems. Our findings provide
support for heightened attention and awareness toward psychological distress in patients with uveitis, as well as remind
clinicians of the significance of disease education.
Furthermore, perfecting the medical guarantee system is particularly important for patients with uveitis.
