## Eligibility criteria (items 5, DTA 6)

We included studies of adults (18 years or older) that evaluated a self-report questionnaire or screening item against a measure of visual function or of need for low vision rehabilitation (LVR), and reported diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity, specificity, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve), validation data (reliability, Rasch or other psychometric analysis) or clinical use. Index tests were brief self-report screening items (for example, the two-item restaurant-menu and keyboard questions from the 100-item acuity survey) and full-length instruments (NEI VFQ-25, VA LV VFQ-48, LVQOL, IVI, GQL-15, Activity Inventory, APEDS-VFQ, GAL-9, VF-14 and the EQ-5D vision bolt-on). Reference standards were measured visual acuity, visual field, contrast sensitivity, a full-length questionnaire, or clinician-judged LVR need. Glaucoma-specific patient-reported outcome studies and guidelines or consensus statements on LVR referral were also eligible. We excluded studies of children only, rehabilitation interventions without questionnaire validation or screening data, studies with no questionnaire or screening item, conference abstracts without usable data, case reports and opinion pieces (opinion pieces were cited for context only), and reports not in English or French. Publication year 2000 onward; any publication status.

For synthesis, studies were grouped by index test (brief screening items versus full-length instruments), by reference standard (visual acuity, visual field, LVR need) and by positivity threshold.

## Effect measures and synthesis (items 12-13, DTA 13-14)

The principal accuracy measures were sensitivity and specificity per participant, with 95% Wilson confidence intervals computed from the counts. Secondary measures were area under the curve and correlation coefficients between questionnaire scores and visual acuity, contrast sensitivity or visual field. Where a paper reported a single-threshold "AUC" that is the mean of sensitivity and specificity, it is shown but not treated as an area under a curve.

**Narrative synthesis (SWiM; Campbell 2020).** Because the included studies used different index tests, reference standards and thresholds, and no comparison had at least four independent studies at a common threshold (the largest group held one independent accuracy dataset and the largest correlation group two), we did not perform a meta-analysis. Studies were grouped by index test and reference standard (SWiM item 1; five groups were specified before tabulation and two emerged during it). Reports from one cohort were collapsed into independent datasets before anything was counted: Wu 2026 (ID 92) and Wu 2024 (ID 128) share one cohort of 385 adults, identified by cross-reference, shared authors, identical sample size and overlapping recruitment years, and count as one dataset; 32 records gave 31 independent datasets [VERIFY: reviewer to confirm the overlap decision]. Results were standardised to sensitivity and specificity with 95% confidence intervals, reconstructed from reported counts or from sensitivity, specificity and prevalence (item 2), and tabulated per study with the threshold and reference standard, with a forest plot without a pooled line and a plot of the studies in ROC space (items 3, 7). Where studies could not be expressed as sensitivity and specificity, we report the measure the study used. We prioritised, for each study, the whole-sample per-person result at the study's own threshold (item 4) and explored heterogeneity by inspecting results by threshold, reference-standard construct, setting, prevalence and mode of administration (item 5); I² was not computed. Certainty of evidence was rated with GRADE for test accuracy without a pooled estimate (item 6). Measurement properties of the full-length instruments were rated with the COSMIN criteria and summarised per instrument with a modified GRADE. Direction-only vote counting was not used. The limitations of this synthesis, in particular its vulnerability to selective emphasis, the lack of a formal estimate and the dependence of the two index items on one research group's cohort, are discussed (item 9). Analyses were run with Python scripts developed with AI assistance (`.claude/skills/`), each run in isolated mode.

## Certainty of evidence (items 15, DTA 24)

We rated the certainty of evidence for the sensitivity and specificity of each index test against each reference standard with the GRADE approach for test accuracy (Schünemann 2008; Murad 2017), starting at high for cross-sectional accuracy studies and downgrading for risk of bias, indirectness, inconsistency, imprecision and publication bias. With one independent dataset per comparison, inconsistency and publication bias could not be assessed, and the ratings describe the range of per-study estimates, not a pooled value. Because no pooled estimate exists, we did not produce a Summary of Findings table with consequences per 1000 people tested, and no pre-test probabilities were chosen. Ratings are given in the text and in `analysis/synthesis/certainty.md`: low for the two Wu items, very low for every other accuracy group, and no evidence for any instrument tested against clinician-judged LVR need.
