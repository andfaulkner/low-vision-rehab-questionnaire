# Glaucoma Today format notes

Issue used: **September/October 2026** (glaucomatoday.com/issues/2026-sept-oct), which is online. Cover theme: refractive considerations in glaucomatous eyes.

Sources read in full (PDF text from assets.bmctoday.net, the "View PDF" link on each article page):

- Feature: Johnson CD, Chapman R, Goldburg S, Qiu M. "Vascular Risk Factors in Normal-Tension Glaucoma." pp 41-45 (`0926GT_Feature_Johnson.pdf`). The only review-style feature in the issue; the closest model for our manuscript.
- Cover Focus: Berdahl JP. "Multifocal and EDOF IOLs: My Rationale for Use in Patients With Glaucoma." pp 31-32 (`0926GT_F1_Berdahl.pdf`).
- Web pages (summary only, via fetch): Sustainability Recommendations for Glaucoma Practice (Practice Pointers), Toric IOLs (Campagna), Premium IOLs (Schehlein), About and Contact pages, issue table of contents.

Limits: the article HTML pages return 403 to scripted download, so the Practice Pointers, Toric and Premium articles were read only as fetch-tool summaries. Quotations below come from the two PDFs. The PDF text layer shows no bold or italic runs, so typography is described from the layout only.

## Length

- Feature (Johnson): about **2,500 words** of text including title, dek, byline, folios and figure captions, from a raw text count of 3,240 words minus 706 for the reference list and author blocks. Four magazine pages with two figures. 24 references.
- Cover Focus (Berdahl): about 885 words before references (2 pages, one sidebar). 5 references.
- Practice Pointers (Sustainability): about 650 words of body text (fetch-tool estimate). 3 references.
- **Target for our manuscript: 2,400 to 2,700 words of body text** (Feature length), plus 20 to 30 references. Hard range for the checklist: 2,200 to 3,000 words excluding references, author block, figure captions and boxes.

## Title and dek

- Title in capitals in print, title case in the web table of contents; no colon-subtitle pattern in the feature. Cover Focus titles use "My Rationale for..." and "When and When Not to Use...".
- The dek is one sentence, 8 to 17 words, ends with a period, states the clinical point rather than the topic. Verbatim: "A practical approach to evaluating patients with progressive disease despite controlled IOP." and "These technologies can be safe and perform well in glaucomatous eyes."
- Byline: "BY CIERA D. JOHNSON; RACHEL CHAPMAN, MD; SAMANTHA GOLDBURG, MD; AND MARY QIU, MD" (semicolons between authors, degrees after names, a trainee without a degree carries none).

## Abstract and key-points box

- **No abstract** in either article.
- The Feature has **no "At a Glance" box**. Its device is a bold-run "Clinical Takeaways" paragraph (2 to 4 sentences) after each topical subsection, and a "Questions to Ask the Patient:" bullet list (3 to 5 bullets) before it. The web edition of Practice Pointers shows a "Key Takeaways" box of 3 bullets; it was not seen in print. Our plan's "At a Glance" box is therefore optional; if used, keep it to 3 bullets of one sentence each.
- Drop-cap first letter; end-of-article square mark after the last sentence.

## Headings

- Used. **Short, ALL-CAPS, unnumbered section heads**, 2 to 6 words, mostly noun phrases or a question, e.g. "IS IT TRULY NTG?", "WHY LOOK BEYOND IOP?", "A TARGETED VASCULAR HISTORY", "PUTTING IT INTO PRACTICE", "CONCLUSION". The introduction has no heading. Third-level heads in capitals under a section ("BP AND NOCTURNAL HYPOTENSION", "SLEEP-DISORDERED BREATHING"). Berdahl uses numbered mini-questions ("No. 1: Is their glaucoma controlled?") in sentence case. Nine to twelve heads in the feature. Every article ends with a CONCLUSION section.
- Methods are not a section in clinical features. The Johnson review has none; its search method is not stated. Put our short Methods paragraph as a sidebar or a short "HOW WE SEARCHED" section before the conclusion (decision for the writing step).

## Authors, affiliations, disclosures

Block at the end of the article, after the references, one per author, name in capitals, then lines each starting with a square bullet:

```
MARY QIU, MD
n Glaucoma specialist and Glaucoma Fellowship Director, Cleveland Clinic Cole Eye Institute, Cleveland
n mary.qiu@gmail.com
n Financial disclosure: Consultant (AbbVie, Iantrek, ...); Medical advisory board (LEP, Nova Eye Medical)
```

(The square bullet extracts as "n".) Role then institution then city (and state if not obvious). A disclosure line is **required for every author**: "Financial disclosure: None" or "Financial disclosure: Consultant (X, Y)" / "Employee (X)" / "Medical advisory board (X)". Berdahl adds "Member, GT Editorial Advisory Board".

## Sidebars, tables, figures

- Sidebar: Berdahl has a shaded "PERSPECTIVE ON MINI-MONOVISION" box of about 100 words by a second named expert, signed with name and affiliation. Editor's cross-reference notes appear in parentheses in the text: "(Editor's note: For more on this topic, see "Toric IOLs: ..." on page 33.)".
- Figures: numbered "Figure 1." with a one-sentence caption, cited in the text as "(Figure 1)". Figure 1 is an algorithm and Figure 2 a boxed list of questions; both are authored graphics, not data plots. Abbreviations are expanded in a caption line ("Abbreviation: CPAP = continuous positive airway pressure."). No numbered tables appear in either article, so tables should be few (one or two) and styled like Figure 2.
- Abbreviations are spelled out on first use, then used.
- Statistics in text: "odds ratios (ORs) of 3.32 (95% CI, 1.84-6.00)", "(hazard ratio, 2.03; 95% CI, 1.55-2.67)", "P = .01" (no leading zero), en-dash ranges, "mm Hg".

## References

- Numbered **by first appearance**, superscript in text after punctuation ("...slow disease progression,1 it is..."); several as "2,3" and "4,5"; the reference list sits at the end in small type, in two columns, before the author block.
- **Authors:** all authors listed up to six; seven or more are cut to the first three then "et al". Surname plus initials without periods, no "and". A group author is given as the group name ("Collaborative Normal-Tension Glaucoma Study Group.").
- Article titles in sentence case, no quotation marks; journal in NLM abbreviation; "Year;volume(issue):pages" with **full** page ranges (101-110, not 101-10); article numbers as given (2025;12:CD015436); the issue is sometimes dropped.
- **DOIs appear** as "doi:10.xxxx/..." with no https://doi.org prefix and no closing period. Every reference in the feature carries one.
- Web sources and "Accessed": none occurs in either article, so the wording is not confirmed from the issue. The style file keeps the AMA wording "Accessed {date}. {URL}".
- Verbatim model entry (Johnson et al, reference 3):

> 3. Zhao J, Solano MM, Oldenburg CE, et al. Prevalence of normal-tension glaucoma in the Chinese population: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Am J Ophthalmol. 2019;199:101-110. doi:10.1016/j.ajo.2018.10.017

A two-author entry (reference 19): "Konieczka K, Flammer J. Treatment of glaucoma patients with Flammer syndrome. J Clin Med. 2021;10(18):4227. doi:10.3390/jcm10184227". A guideline (reference 8): "Gedde SJ, Bowden EC, Challa P, et al. Primary open-angle glaucoma preferred practice pattern. Ophthalmology. 2026;133(4):P1-P103. doi:10.1016/j.ophtha.2025.12.029".

## AI-assistance policy

None found. The About and Contact pages carry no author guidelines, no policy on AI and no ethics statement. The footer "Submissions" link opens an email to the Editor-in-Chief (subject "GT Article Submission"), so submission is by email to the editorial office. Disclose AI use in the Methods paragraph anyway (plan rule 7) and ask the editor which wording they prefer.

## Verbatim model: title, dek and first paragraph

Source: Johnson CD, Chapman R, Goldburg S, Qiu M. Glaucoma Today, September/October 2026, p 41.

> VASCULAR RISK FACTORS IN NORMAL-TENSION GLAUCOMA
>
> A practical approach to evaluating patients with progressive disease despite controlled IOP.
>
> BY CIERA D. JOHNSON; RACHEL CHAPMAN, MD; SAMANTHA GOLDBURG, MD; AND MARY QIU, MD
>
> A familiar clinical scenario in glaucoma practice is the patient with normal-tension glaucoma (NTG) whose disease continues to progress despite seemingly adequate IOP control. The instinct is often to pursue an even lower target IOP. Although IOP reduction remains the only intervention proven to slow disease progression,1 it is worth considering whether another factor could be contributing to the patient's optic nerve vulnerability.

(Reference marker "1" is superscript in print.) Structure to copy: a clinical scenario opening, a statement of what the article will do ("This review outlines a practical approach to...") at the end of the introduction, topical sections each ending in questions or takeaways, a "PUTTING IT INTO PRACTICE" section, then a CONCLUSION that ends with a one-line message.

## Things the magazine asks that the plan doc does not mention

- A financial-disclosure line for every author is mandatory in the author block.
- Emails for the authors are printed (a contact line per author).
- The feature has no abstract or At-a-Glance box; the plan's box is optional. "Clinical Takeaways" after each section and a questions list fit our technician work-up protocol well.
- Section heads are ALL CAPS and unnumbered; the plan's numbered outline must be rewritten as short heads, with no Introduction heading.
- Page-condensed ranges are not used.
- Submission is by email to the Editor-in-Chief; there are no published author guidelines or AI policy.
- References are numbered by first appearance in the text and the feature carries about 24 of them.
