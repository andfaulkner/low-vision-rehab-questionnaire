Journal of Test Ophthalmology 2019; 12(3): 101-106

A single question to identify visual impairment in community-dwelling older adults

Jane Doe, Alan Roe, Priya Nair

Purpose: To estimate the accuracy of one self-report question for detecting presenting visual
impairment among adults aged 65 and over attending a community health fair.
Methods: Cross-sectional study. Participants answered the question "Do you have difficulty
reading ordinary newspaper print, even with your glasses?" (yes/no) before presenting
distance visual acuity was measured in each eye with a Snellen chart at 6 m. Visual impairment
was defined as presenting visual acuity worse than 6/12 in the better eye.
Results: 240 adults took part. Sixty (25%) had visual impairment. The question had a
sensitivity of 0.75 and a specificity of 0.80.
Conclusion: A single question identifies three quarters of older adults with presenting visual
impairment and is brief enough for routine use.

INTRODUCTION
Uncorrected refractive error and untreated cataract remain the leading causes of visual
impairment in older adults, and many affected people are not in contact with eye services.
A question that can be asked by non-specialists would allow targeted referral. We tested one
such question against measured acuity.

METHODS
Design and participants
We recruited consecutive attenders aged 65 years or older at a community health fair held in
Somewhere, Ontario, in May 2018. People who could not communicate in English or who had a
cognitive impairment documented by a carer were excluded. Of 262 people approached, 240
agreed to take part and completed both the question and the acuity test. Mean age was 74.2
years (SD 6.1; range 65 to 93) and 138 (57.5%) were women. Ocular diagnoses were not
collected.

Index test
Before any vision test, a trained volunteer asked: "Do you have difficulty reading ordinary
newspaper print, even with your glasses?" The answer was recorded as yes or no. A "yes" answer
was taken as screen positive.

[page 3]
Reference standard
Presenting distance visual acuity (with the spectacles the participant was wearing, if any)
was measured in each eye with a standard Snellen chart at 6 m by an optometrist who did not
know the answer to the question. Visual impairment was defined as acuity worse than 6/12
(20/40) in the better eye.

Analysis
We calculated sensitivity, specificity and their 95% confidence intervals, treating the
optometrist's classification as the reference standard. Analyses were done in Stata 15.

Eighty-one participants answered "yes" to the question. The question took well under a
minute to ask but we did not time it.

DISCUSSION
One question detected 75% of older adults with presenting visual impairment. Specificity was
moderate, so one in five unimpaired people would be referred. Our sample came from a single
health fair and may over-represent people with concerns about their vision.
